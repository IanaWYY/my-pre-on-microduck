01 / THE TECH

It doesn't just follow instructions.
It learns how to move.

Microduck is a small biped robot built around a powerful idea: train a behaviour, test it, and improve it through experience.

01 — LEARN

Reinforcement learning

An agent tries actions, receives rewards or penalties, and improves its policy over many attempts. The goal is not to memorize one fixed sequence, but to learn a strategy for acting.

02 — PRACTISE

Simulation first

Training in a physics simulator allows many experiments without repeatedly risking the physical robot. The simulated world can be reset and run at scale.

03 — TRANSFER

Sim-to-real

A learned policy is deployed on the real robot. Differences between simulation and reality still matter, so the system may need refinement and retraining.

04 — IMPROVE

Policies are shareable

A movement policy can be retrained, evaluated and shared. The robot becomes a platform for experimenting with behaviour, not just a pre-programmed toy.

The interesting shift: from writing every movement by hand to training a system that learns a way to achieve a goal.
A USEFUL ANALOGY

Learning to speak vs.
learning to stand.

Both involve learning patterns from experience, but their training objectives and outputs are different.

LLMs

Predict the next token

Large language models commonly learn statistical patterns in text by predicting tokens. Their outputs are sequences of language tokens.

Robot policies

Choose the next action

A robot policy maps observations or states to actions. Reinforcement learning can improve those actions based on reward and task performance.

Important distinction: a Transformer is a model architecture, while reinforcement learning is a family of learning methods. They are not direct opposites; a Transformer can also be used inside a system trained with reinforcement learning.

02 / THE TERMS

Embodied AI vs.
Physical AI.

The terms overlap, but they point our attention toward slightly different parts of the same challenge: intelligent systems acting in the physical world.

THE INTELLIGENCE LENS

Embodied AI

Often emphasizes the relationship between a body, perception, action and the environment. Intelligence is shaped by interacting with the world, rather than processing information in isolation.

Body Perception Action
THE SYSTEMS LENS

Physical AI

Often emphasizes AI systems that understand, predict and act in physical environments, including the engineering stack needed to train, simulate, deploy and operate them.

Simulation Compute Deployment
Not two competing scientific categories. They are overlapping ways of framing AI that connects to the physical world.

In Chinese technology conversations, “具身智能” is a particularly visible term. NVIDIA often uses “Physical AI” to describe the wider technology and industrial ecosystem around real-world AI. The exact boundary depends on context.

03 / THE ECOSYSTEM

Three players.
Different pieces of the puzzle.

Microduck is interesting not only as a robot, but also as an example of how hardware, open-source communities and accelerated computing connect.

Pollen Robotics

The robot and robotics expertise. Pollen develops the hardware and robotics platform that lets developers experiment with real physical movement.

Hugging Face

The open ecosystem. Models, datasets, code and community infrastructure help people share experiments and build on one another's work. Hugging Face acquired Pollen Robotics in 2025.

NVIDIA

The computing and simulation stack. GPUs, accelerated computing and simulation tools can support the training and development of Physical AI systems. NVIDIA is not the parent company of Pollen Robotics.

04 / THE STRATEGY

Why show a tiny duck
on a big AI stage?

A physical robot makes an abstract technology stack tangible. It gives people something they can watch, test, train and potentially build on.

From chips to outcomes

AI infrastructure is easier to understand when you can see the chain from computing and simulation to a robot performing a task.

Open development

A small, accessible robot can invite developers and researchers to experiment with policies instead of treating robotics as a closed industrial system.

Europe's industrial context

Robotics connects AI to Europe's engineering, manufacturing and industrial technology base. That is a strategic context, not proof of any single corporate motive.

A demo is a starting point

A successful demonstration shows what is possible in a particular setup. It does not, by itself, prove that a robot can reliably handle every unfamiliar real-world situation.

05 / THE VIBE-CODING CONNECTION

Code is no longer
the whole story.

In vibe coding, a developer can describe an outcome, orchestrate tools, test what was generated and refine it. In robotics, the same loop becomes even more tangible: the output may move something in the real world.

01 / DEFINE

Describe a goal

What should the system accomplish?

02 / TRAIN

Build a policy

Learn a behaviour in simulation or through experience.

03 / TEST

Observe reality

Check whether the behaviour works outside the ideal setup.

04 / REFINE

Repeat the loop

Evaluate, adjust, retrain and share what works.

The developer's role shifts from specifying every detail to directing, evaluating and improving a system's behaviour.
