The key distinction

LLMs learn sequences.
Robot policies learn actions.

Both optimize parameterized models, but their objectives differ. A language model is commonly pretrained to predict the next token; an RL policy learns actions that maximize expected reward under an environment's dynamics.

Transformer is a neural-network architecture. Reinforcement learning is a family of learning and decision-making methods. They are not competing labels: a robot policy can itself use a Transformer.

Sequence prediction Action selection Feedback loops
The bigger shift: AI outputs are no longer only words, images, or code. They can become actions that change the physical world.
02 / The language

Embodied AI
≠ just a buzzword.

“Embodied AI” is prominent in Chinese AI discourse. “Physical AI” is a framing NVIDIA uses to describe the technologies and industry it wants to enable.

A

Embodied AI

Research lens: how does intelligence emerge through a body, perception, action, and interaction with an environment?

Focus: learning, generalization, sensing, action, and the agent–environment loop.

B

Physical AI

Industry lens: how can AI understand, simulate, and act in the physical world?

Focus: models, simulation, computing, robotics, autonomous systems, and deployment.

C

The overlap

Both concern AI interacting with the physical world. The boundary is not strict, and the terms are not exact synonyms.

Useful distinction, not a universal academic definition.

03 / The industry stack

Why these
three players?

Pollen Robotics, Hugging Face, and NVIDIA occupy different — and complementary — layers of the AI ecosystem.

NVIDIA
Compute + platform

Build and run the workflow. GPUs, accelerated computing, Isaac tools, simulation, cloud training, and deployment infrastructure.

Hugging Face
Open ecosystem

Share and reuse AI. Models, datasets, developer tools, collaboration, and an open community extended into robotics.

Pollen Robotics
Robot hardware

Give AI a body. Mechanical design, motors, sensors, control systems, and a real robot on which to test learned behaviour.

Developers
Experiment + build

Make new behaviours. Train, evaluate, modify, and publish policies rather than treating every factory behaviour as a fixed feature.

From a product to a platform

Microduck's SDK, simulator, and reinforcement-learning stack are published openly. Its behaviours can be retrained and shared — making the robot a learning platform, not only a finished gadget.

Hugging Face acquired Pollen Robotics in 2025. NVIDIA is not Pollen's parent company: its role in the GTC demonstration was to show how NVIDIA tools can support the training and deployment workflow.

Same underlying robot. Different shells. A platform designed to be retrained.
04 / The strategic signal

Why did Jensen
Huang show it?

The keynote was not simply a product launch. Microduck made NVIDIA's wider Physical AI story visible.

01

Make the stack tangible

A small robot demonstrates a complete workflow: simulation, cloud training, local evaluation, then deployment to real hardware.

02

Expand beyond GPUs

NVIDIA's strategic offer is not just chips. It wants developers to use its computing, software, simulation, and deployment ecosystem.

03

Connect to Europe's industry

European strengths in precision engineering, mechatronics, automotive, and manufacturing make the region a meaningful Physical AI market.

A memorable demo — but a demo is not proof of general-purpose autonomy.
The GTC Berlin connection

Three computers, one workflow

1. Cloud: large-scale training runs on NVIDIA computing systems.

2. Digital twin: simulate and evaluate behaviours before deploying them.

3. Physical robot: run the learned policy on the actual Microduck.

At GTC Berlin on October 21, 2026, Huang used Microduck to connect NVIDIA's platform story with the broader message that Europe should build AI capabilities, not only consume AI services.

Tech Radar reading: the next competition is not only about better models. It is about the stack that turns models into reliable action.
